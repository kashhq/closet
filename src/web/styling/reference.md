# Styling: reference

Moved out of `AGENTS.md` (#723) so the auto-loaded doc keeps only invariants, gotchas and ownership. Not auto-loaded: read it before changing this area. Where it disagrees with the code or a spec, they win.

- **Reads** (`queries.ts`, scalar subqueries read together by `reads.ts`; #163): `roleWindowsSql` is the windows whatever the wardrobe holds: window functions rank and count each role (`categoryRole` as a SQL `case`, from `builtInCategoriesOf`) and return the role's first `STRIP_PAGE` (10) and `STRIP_PAGE` either side of each chosen garment, ranked (#996: not the run from the newest down to it, a whole role per strip at 500 garments); each row shows the run around its own garment (`windowAround`, `rows.ts`). A strip's sentinels (`intersect`, a horizontal scroller) fetch `GET /styling/garments?role=&before=<id>` (older, at its end) or `&after=<id>` (newer, right before the window's first garment when it is deep; one of the two, a `oneOf` on the query), keyset; snap-strip.js keeps the chosen item centred as a newer page lands before it. **Chosen is judged in SQL as `(garment, row's role)` pairs** (`Chosen`), so a posted garment reaches its strip exactly when `checkedStates` keeps it (the wardrobe's, of that role; a recategorised one stretches nothing), and `?outfit=` reaches its garments through the outfit's id. `savedOutfitSql` (an outfit, its owner's), `rowGarmentsSql` (what posted rows may carry: see Include picks), capsules' `capsuleRefSql` (the `?capsule=` check). **Statements** (production pays ~114 ms each, #156; `styling-statements.spec.ts` pins them): after the session (which reads a grantee's share with it, #170), `stripsReads` is one `selectScalars` statement with the checks, the windows, the capsule menu and a grantee's switcher name: the page without `?with=`, "Add row" and a refused Save's page are that one statement (a trip's `aimIdeas` beside it). "Style this" and Shuffle need two, as the windows must reach the idea: `ideaReads` first (the checks, the posted rows' garments and the lockable ones, `styledGarmentsSql`, with ideasFor's own statement through `ideasColumns`/`ideasFrom`, or over a share `closetGarmentsSql` for the pure `browseIdea`), then `stripsReads`. The checks ride in the statement and are judged after it (`checkedCapsule`, `checkedOutfit`, `checkedStates`): reading before a capsule check is safe, since `inCapsule` matches nothing of another wardrobe. A grantee's statements never name `garment_wear`, `outfit_slot` or `generator_avoid` (the locked garments are read without their last-worn day). The price: with more than one bad parameter the refusal named may differ from before (a rows 400 now comes before a capsule 404; a trip's 404 before the capsule's on Shuffle and "Style this"); each alone refuses as it did.
- **Include picks** (`?picks=1`, #335 part B, the Muse epic; `docs/plans/2026-10-05-muse-suggestions.md` §4 E; it replaced the plan's `?plan=`, #273): one switch, `PicksToggle` at the top of the rows (`role="switch"`), off by default, so the bare page stays byte-stable. **Offered only where a pick could be saved** (`Wardrobe.picksOffered`): never over a shared wardrobe, with a destination (`scopeOf`: `?for=` plans or packs the outfit) or in an edit of a planned or packed outfit (`StripsReads.editingHeld`, read in the strips' statement: such an outfit takes no pick, so its strips carry none and its rows drop one, by the writer's own `offeredIntoSql`). There it is neither shown nor read. **Nor while one's closet cannot be dressed** (the first-run gate, #666: `closetCanDress` over `closetKindsSql`, read in the strips' statement, `StripsReads.canDress`; the closet's, whatever the capsule), unless picks are on already. With no rows (an empty capsule, or picks on over an empty closet) it is a plain link (`PicksLink`), as picks alone may make rows. On, the garments offered to style with (`picksSql`: `offeredToStyle`, still wanted and not under a need set aside, the owner's own wishlist items too) join their role's strip **first**, badged with the to-buy `GarmentMark` (`data-mark="to-buy"`), **a need's options side by side** (by need, newest first, then Muse's rank; then lone picks and own items, newest first). They are a window's `picks` (`withPicks`, `rows.ts`), not the cycle: `count` and `moreBefore` ignore them, `freshStates` opens on nothing, a pick least of all, a role the closet lacks still gets a row. They ride in `stripsReads`' one statement (`styling-statements.spec.ts`). **What posted rows may carry is one rule, `rowGarmentsSql`** (`RowCarry`, from the scope by `carryOf`): closet garments; with picks on, also what `offeredToStyle` offers; while editing a saved outfit, also its own garments. It is the outfit writer's own rule (`slotMayNameSql`), read by every read of posted rows (the page, Shuffle, "Add row", a refused Save's page), so a row never holds what Save would refuse and a chosen pick survives every round trip (Shuffle never draws one; a locked one stays). A Save reads its garments only for their categories, in posted order (`savedGarments`), and `insertSlots` judges them under the owner lock. **The switch keeps the composition**: it asks `GET /styling/row` with the rows (`hx-include`, `picks` by `hx-vals`), answering the rows (it sits at their top, the `picks` field with them) and pushing the page's address with `picks` flipped. **Every link outside the rows that carries the page's state is answered with them**: the capsule menu (`ScopeMenu`, `#styling-scope`) comes out of band in the same fragment, its capsules in the same statement (`stripsReads`' `capsules`). The rest outside the rows carry no `picks` (Back is `returnTo`; a destination has no switch; the empty state has no rows to swap). **Save with picks on** (`picks` posted) is a slot write, `saveWithPicks` through `createOutfit` (not `pickTo`): the outfit is incomplete until its pieces are bought (Outfits, Incomplete outfits), once however often posted; with the sheet's day an incomplete one is refused (`OutfitIncomplete`, the page again with its pieces and "Bought it" links, rows kept); with a destination posted, a 400. An edit's Save carries any of the owner's garments, picks on or not. The outfit page's Edit in Styling opens an incomplete outfit with `picks=1`. Behaviour: `styling-picks.spec.ts` (integration and Playwright).
- **The browser** (the shared snap strip, `src/web/strip/AGENTS.md`: `SnapStrip`/`snapItem` in `styling-row.tsx`, the observer in `public/js/snap-strip.js`; `public/js/styling.js` adds the locks through the observer's `snap-strip:choose` and `snap-strip:watch` events; an inline module per visit plus `htmx:load`): an IntersectionObserver per strip with its margins pulled to a line down the middle writes the crossing item (`data-snap-value`) into the row's hidden `garmentId`. **No touch handlers**: swiping is the browser's scrolling (`test/styling.spec.ts` checks the app has no non-passive touch or wheel listener). A tap on a peeking neighbour centres it (a click listener in the **capture** phase: the garments are boosted links, whose htmx handler on the element runs before a bubbling document listener); a tap on the chosen one opens its page. Each row posts `role`, `garmentId` and `lock` hidden fields in step; the lock is daisyUI's swap (the label is the control); styling.js's `setLocked` (a document `change` listener) owns a toggle: its row's `lock` field, `checked` mirrored into its attribute (htmx's history snapshot is innerHTML, which drops the property) and the neighbours' `inert`. Items snap one at a time (`snap-always`).
- **What a row shows is CSS off its state** (#106; `styling-row.tsx`, Tailwind `group/row` and `group/item` variants, no script): the `[data-selected]` item's plinth wears an inset ink ring (inset: the strip clips anything outside). **A locked row is frozen** (owner decision on #106): `:has(.styling-lock:checked)` gives its strip `overflow-x: hidden` and `touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom` (the page still scrolls vertically and zooms), its ring turns accent and its neighbours' plinths fade. **Its neighbours are `inert`** (#146; `itemState`): a frozen strip cannot reveal a focused neighbour (the scroll guard snapped it back and the focus sat off-screen), so they leave the tab order, the accessibility tree (the listbox offers the chosen garment alone, beside a checked "Lock <role>") and hit testing (a tap on one lands on the strip: nothing centres, nothing opens). The server renders it (`?with=`, Shuffle), so a row opened locked is right from the first paint; `setLocked` keeps it in step, and `watch()` makes a strip's next page inert as it lands (`GET /styling/garments` does not carry the lock). `inert`, not `tabindex="-1"`: that would leave unchoosable options in the accessibility tree and still reachable by a screen reader's cursor or a click. The observer never chooses in a locked row: whatever else scrolls it (a script's `scrollTo` still moves a hidden-overflow strip, which is also why centring on load works for `?with=`) is scrolled back to the chosen item. Unlocking swipes on from the same item; Shuffle keeps a locked row (it is left out of the idea) and it stays frozen. `test/styling.spec.ts` proves a wheel leaves a locked strip's `scrollLeft` alone at 390 px, and that Tab reaches only a locked row's chosen garment (Firefox also stops on the strip itself, a scroll container), and every item once unlocked.
- **Desktop (#468, `docs/plans/2026-11-12-desktop-styling-and-calendar.md` §2; on `panel` since #1073, `docs/plans/2026-11-16-desktop-layout.md` §4): one markup, CSS from `lg`.** The page is `PageShell layout="panel"` (`stylingLayout`; the empty state stays a `wide` single column) and `StylingAside` its aside (`#styling-aside`, an `<aside>` named "The outfit", `asideBelowLg: 'bar'`; Layout's A page's frame): from lg the 20rem column beside the body (the header, the form and "Add row"), sticky under the app bar and capped to the window, holding a `<details>` whose summary is `StylingPreview` (named by an sr-only span, no `aria-label`; the summary is inert from lg, `open` untouched; `--app-bar-h` in main.css), then `StylingFit` outside the summary (so its notes are announced), then `StylingActions` (Shuffle and Save, one line at both sizes, Save taking the rest); Shuffle and Save read the preview's state through `group/bar`, a box-less wrapper inside the aside. The `<details>` is `min-h-0`, scrolling inside, at both sizes, so a tall outfit gives way before the fit line or the buttons leave the window. **On a phone the aside is the bar above the dock** (#664): sticky, not fixed, so, in the flow after the rows and "Add row", it rides on the dock while they scroll and lands below the last at their end: it never covers a row however tall the chips or the opened preview make it (the page pads only the dock). z-10, under "Add row"'s z-20 menu. Its strip is the preview in one line (the `PREVIEW_*` classes); a tap on the summary opens it into the desktop's stack: each class of that whole shape is one `preview-whole:` class (main.css's custom variant: the `group` details open, or from lg), so the two shapes cannot drift (#1073; they were `group-open:` and `lg:` twins). **The rows' tile from lg is `regular`'s w-28** (`small` steps up to it, Snap strip's `size`): seven rows are 1432 px at 1440 instead of 1768 (never smaller than the phone's garment tile, so not the design's 1150), and the sticky aside keeps Shuffle and Save beside the rows being chosen in; the strips stay centred choices (owner decision 2 of the desktop design). The page carries one `lg:` of its own (the summary's chevron). **The preview** (`#styling-preview`, `aria-hidden`): a slot per row in row order (`data-preview-slot` = the row's index, `data-preview-garment` = the chosen id or ''), worn roles stacked, `ridesAlong` roles in a line at the foot, each the chosen garment's `GarmentLook` (the plinth's own markup) or empty (`empty:hidden`). The server draws it (bare, `?with=`, `?outfit=`, a refused Save) and `renderRows` sends it out of band with every rows answer (Shuffle, "Add row", the picks switch), no statement added; between them styling.js copies the chosen item's `[data-styling-plinth]` children into its slot on `snap-strip:chosen`, so it follows a swipe offline. Not `OutfitCollage` (it lays out by the roles present, which only the server knows).
